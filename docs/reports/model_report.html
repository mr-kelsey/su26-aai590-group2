Capstone · presence-side modelling

Where San Francisco’s evening economy moves on game nights

Two counterfactual models over a 452-cell citywide grid, a crossover test against a second arena, and a dollar figure reconciled exactly to California tax receipts.

Window 2023-01-02 → 2025-12-31 Panel 11,878,560 cell-hours Games 246 Giants · 211 Chase Branch feature/citywide-spatiotemporal-nowcast

Every figure below is measured, not illustrative. Where a result was overturned by a later run, the overturned version is stated rather than quietly dropped.

The substrate

452 blocks, drawn where the city actually trades

San Francisco is cut into 250 m cells; a cell enters the model only if it holds at least ten points of interest. That keeps 452 cells covering 14,467 POIs — 81.9% of the city’s total — and drops the sparse residential fringe rather than modelling noise. The cell size is not a convenience: adjacent cells correlate 0.445 on hourly residuals while the correlation length runs 750 m–1 km, so 250 m sits where neighbours are informative but not redundant.

The shape of the city emerges from the data alone — no basemap is drawn. Cells exist only where commerce does, which is why the Bay, the Presidio and Golden Gate Park appear as voids.

Design

What the models actually predict

Both models answer one question: what would activity have been in this cell, this hour, had there been no event? They are trained on control hours only and never see an event, so the residual on a game hour is causally readable rather than partly fitted.

Neither is autoregressive. Feeding recent activity would be fatal — during a game the last hours already contain the effect, so the model would predict the inflated level and the residual, which is the measurement, would collapse toward zero. Instead both see calendar, weather, cell composition, and a rolling baseline built from prior control days at three lookback depths.

Because no input depends on recent activity, the models have no forecast horizon. Every input is knowable ahead of time; only weather forecast quality bounds how far out they stay useful.

Model 1

Gradient-boosted counterfactual

A pooled LightGBM over all 452 cells, with cell identity as a categorical feature. One row per cell-hour, no spatial structure whatsoever. Trained on 4.2 M control hours, validated on held-out control hours where ground truth genuinely exists.

The lookback that mattered

The single largest gain came not from architecture but from the temporal reference. A baseline averaging the 8 most recent same-weekday control days sounds local, but because 47% of candidate days are events and get skipped, it reached back a median 112 calendar days on game days — as far as 203. A September Thursday was partly baselined on Thursdays in May.

Root-mean-square error turns out to have an interior optimum near k=3–4, and combining depths beats any single one. Replacing k=8 with three depths cut test error 10.9% — roughly five times what the graph contributed.

Model 2

Spatiotemporal graph network

A learned embedding per cell, dilated causal convolution along time, and graph convolution across the 452 cells — the architecture family used for freeway sensor networks, at comparable scale. It receives the identical feature set, splits and masking, so any gap between the two models is attributable to the graph rather than to feature engineering.

Three edge families were ablated against a no-graph control whose adjacency is all zeros. Graph convolution keeps separate self and neighbour weights, so the network can drive the graph to zero if the edges are noise — which matters, because forcing a useless graph to inject noise would have misread the result.

What the ablation says

The graph carries real signal, and in the direction predicted before fitting: edges weighted by the measured correlation function help, while raw unweighted contiguity actively hurts. Citywide, adjacent cells correlate only 0.114 — that is precisely the regime where naive adjacency is noise but weighted edges are not.

The network confirms it independently. Neighbour-weight norms settle ~2% below their initialisation, meaning the model neither leans on the graph nor rejects it outright.

The gradient-boosted model wins by 16%. At this scale, cell identity plus a well-specified temporal baseline carries most of the signal, and 2,913 training windows is thin for a neural model to recover that through a 32-dimensional embedding — whereas the boosted trees get 452 cell offsets almost for free. An architecture that cannot beat the simpler benchmark is a result worth reporting, not a failure to bury.

Result

The effect, and how far it reaches

Effects are read as a difference-in-differences on model residuals — game-hour residual minus contemporaneous control-hour residual — which cancels the vendor measurement drift documented below. Inference clusters at the day, because cell-hours within a day are heavily correlated and naive cell-hour intervals overstate significance roughly threefold.

Activity rises three hours before first pitch and stays elevated about four hours after, peaking before the game starts — pre-game dining, then post-game lingering. The event window is roughly eight hours, not three.

Beyond 2 km the effect is indistinguishable from zero. The rest of the city does not fall while the ballpark district rises, so the near-field lift is net new activity rather than spending relocated from elsewhere in San Francisco. That was the question the citywide geography was adopted to answer.

Generalisation

Does the effect follow the venue, or the neighbourhood?

With one venue this is unanswerable. Any model would happily attribute a Mission Bay quirk to the ballpark, and a held-out time split could never catch it — a model that has simply memorised where Oracle Park is would pass that test comfortably.

Chase Center sits 1,188 m away with 211 event days that do not coincide with a Giants game. The 45 days that do coincide are dropped rather than controlled: assigning a shared day to either venue would beg the question. Their 0–500 m rings share no cells, so each treatment can be estimated in both venues’ distance frames.

The diagonal is enormous and the off-diagonal is null. Each venue’s own inner ring responds to its own events and ignores the other’s, 1.2 km away. That is the result the generic-neighbourhood explanation cannot produce.

The geometry also checks itself: each venue appears in the other’s 1–2 km band (+9.8% and +5.6%) — exactly where something 1,188 m distant has to land.

The +648.7% is not a bug, and it is the more interesting number. Chase’s inner ring is a single qualifying cell in newer, low-density Mission Bay development whose commerce is almost purely event-driven — near-dead on a normal Tuesday, packed on an event night. Oracle Park’s district carries a substantial independent SoMa economy that trades regardless. The gap between +43.8% and +648.7% is a real difference in baseline density, not a measurement artifact — though resting on one cell, Chase’s interval deserves more weight than its centre.

Dollars

From presence to taxable spend

California reports food-service sales tax quarterly for San Francisco — and because SF is a consolidated city-county, that grain is the city. Those quarterly totals are distributed across days following the citywide food-visit indicator, reconciling to each observed quarter exactly (maximum relative error 4.8×10−15 over 12 quarters and $14.18 B). Daily SF food-service spend averages $12.94 M.

Three ways this could have been inflated

Evening share applied. Effects are estimated on hours 16–23, so they reach only the 34.5% of daily activity in that window. Applying them to whole days would have roughly tripled the figure.

Attributable fraction is e/(1+e), not e. Observed activity already contains the effect; using the raw uplift would overstate by (1+e).

The null band enters as zero, not as its noisy point estimate.

Note where the money actually comes from: the 1–2 km and 2–4 km bands supply two-thirds of the total on effects of just +4.5% and +1.2%, because their dollar base is large. The dramatic 0–500 m band holds only 1.32% of the city’s food spend and contributes 23%.

$2.57 per attendee will look low beside published impact studies, and that is the point. This excludes retail, lodging, parking, transport, every in-stadium concession, and all indirect and induced activity — and the tax data counts taxable transactions only. It is a floor on a narrow, well-anchored slice, which is the opposite of how event impact studies usually fail.

Caveats

What is validated, and what is not

A measurement drift sits under everything. Person-hours per visit slide 27% across the window, monotonically. Each quarter-over-quarter step stays inside a 15% tolerance gate, so a per-quarter check passes every step while the level drifts badly. Raw levels are therefore not comparable across years; only contrasts against contemporaneous controls are.

An earlier displacement finding was withdrawn. Estimated on the 39 games in the test split alone, activity beyond 4 km appeared to fall 6.0%. Re-estimated on all 246 games it is +0.2% (p=0.69) — a small-sample artifact, recorded here rather than dropped.

The dollar figure is validated only at quarterly grain. The disaggregation reconciles exactly to each observed quarter, but everything within a quarter is principled interpolation carrying quantified-but-unverifiable uncertainty. A daily number is not a measured number.

Velocity drifts, and that bars two things. Citywide food visits are flat (+2%) across 2023–2026 while tax receipts rise 23% — nearly all price, not volume, with dollars-per-visit up 21%. Quarter-by-quarter reconciliation absorbs this, so the disaggregation holds; but velocity cannot be read as a structural parameter, and nothing here may be extrapolated beyond the anchor window.

The estimates are robust to model quality. Improving the counterfactual by 10.9% moved the headline effect by roughly one percentage point — which is the difference-in-differences design working as intended, and which bounds how much a better model could change the conclusions.
